## Overview & Definitions

### What is SIBO?

Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth (SIBO) is a condition characterized by an abnormal increase in bacterial population in the small intestine. The small intestine normally has relatively few bacteria compared to the colon; when colonic-type bacteria proliferate there, it leads to symptoms and malabsorption.

**Key Points:**
- Estimated 14-40% of IBS patients also have SIBO
- New ICD-10 code (K82.11) established in 2024 for improved tracking
- About 70% of all IBS cases may be SIBO/IMO cases
- Symptoms overlap significantly with other GI disorders

### Common Symptoms

- Bloating and abdominal distension
- Abdominal pain/discomfort
- Diarrhea or constipation (depending on type)
- Gas and flatulence
- Nausea
- Malabsorption (B12, iron, fat-soluble vitamins)
- Fatigue
- Brain fog

## Types of Intestinal Overgrowth

Research has distinguished **three distinct variations** of intestinal overgrowth based on the gases produced:

### 1. Hydrogen-Dominant SIBO (H2-SIBO)

- **Organisms:** Overgrowth of hydrogen-producing bacteria
- **Primary symptom pattern:** Diarrhea-predominant, similar to IBS-D
- **Diagnostic threshold:** Rise in H2 of ≥20 ppm above baseline within 90 minutes
- **Treatment:** Rifaximin is the preferred antibiotic

### 2. Intestinal Methanogen Overgrowth (IMO)

- **Organisms:** Overgrowth of methane-producing archaea, primarily *Methanobrevibacter smithii*
- **Key distinction:** Archaea are NOT bacteria - they belong to a separate biological domain
- **Primary symptom pattern:** Constipation-predominant, similar to IBS-C (methane slows intestinal transit)
- **Diagnostic threshold:** Methane ≥10 ppm at any point during testing
- **ICD-10 code:** K63.829 (established October 2023)
- **Treatment:** Combination of rifaximin + neomycin shows 87% efficacy vs 28-33% for single antibiotic
- **Note:** IMO can occur independently of hydrogen-SIBO; the presence of hydrogen-producing commensals is sufficient

### 3. Hydrogen Sulfide SIBO / Intestinal Sulfide Overproduction (ISO)

- **Organisms:** Sulfide-producing bacteria including *Desulfovibrio*, *Bilophila*, and *Fusobacterium*
- **Primary symptom pattern:** Diarrhea-predominant, associated with IBS-D
- **Effects:** H2S can relax smooth muscle and may be pro-inflammatory in excess
- **Diagnostic threshold:** H2S ≥3 ppm at any point during testing
- **Testing challenge:** Up to 25% of patients with normal H2/CH4 tests may have elevated H2S
- **Only test available:** Trio-Smart breath test (measures all three gases)

## Diagnosis & Testing

### Breath Testing

Breath tests remain the most common non-invasive diagnostic method.

#### Test Substrates

| Substrate | Sensitivity | Specificity | Best For |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|----------|
| **Glucose** | 54.5% | 83.2% | Proximal SIBO (fewer false positives) |
| **Lactulose** | 42.0% | 70.6% | Distal SIBO (reaches further) |

**Trade-offs:**
- Glucose is mostly absorbed before reaching the colon → high specificity, low false positives
- Glucose may miss distal SIBO (where overgrowth is in the far end of the small intestine)
- Lactulose can reach further but may produce false positives from colonic bacteria

#### North American Consensus Diagnostic Criteria

| Gas | Threshold | Timing |
|-----|-----------|--------|
| Hydrogen (H2) | ≥20 ppm rise from baseline | Within 90 minutes |
| Methane (CH4) | ≥10 ppm | Any point during test |
| Hydrogen Sulfide (H2S) | ≥3 ppm | Any point during test |

#### Available Tests

1. **Standard Breath Tests** - Measure H2 and CH4 only
2. **Trio-Smart** - Only test measuring all three gases (H2, CH4, H2S)
   - Note: Limited peer-reviewed clinical validity evidence as of 2023
   - Recent 2025 DDW studies validating accuracy

### Gold Standard (Invasive)

**Small Bowel Aspirate and Culture**
- Considered the diagnostic gold standard
- Rarely used due to invasive nature
- Involves endoscopy to collect fluid from small intestine

### Emerging Methods

- Capsule-based testing
- Urine-based testing
- Advanced molecular assays (microbiome sequencing)
- Increased *Enterobacteriaceae* correlates with SIBO diagnosis and symptom severity

## Treatment Approaches

### Pharmaceutical Antibiotics

#### For Hydrogen-SIBO

| Antibiotic | Notes |
|------------|-------|
| **Rifaximin (Xifaxan)** | Preferred first-line; non-systemic, stays in gut |
| Metronidazole | Alternative option |
| Ciprofloxacin | Alternative option |

- Rifaximin alone: 50-60% eradication rate
- Rifaximin + probiotics: ~86% eradication rate

#### For IMO (Methane)

**Combination therapy is essential:**
- Rifaximin + Neomycin: **87% eradication rate**
- Single antibiotic (rifaximin OR neomycin alone): 28-33% eradication rate

#### Key Challenge: Relapse

- Up to **43.7% of patients relapse within 9 months** of completing antibiotic treatment
- Long-term management strategy is essential

### Herbal Antimicrobials

A 2024 randomized clinical trial (Nutrients journal) found:
- Herbal antimicrobials + low-FODMAP diet + probiotics improved symptoms more effectively than standard antibiotics + low-FODMAP diet
- Also helped clear SIBO
- Clinical remission rates higher in the intervention group, especially for CH4-SIBO

**Common herbal antimicrobials:**
- Berberine-containing herbs
- Oregano oil
- Allicin (garlic extract)
- Neem

**Important:** Should be used under guidance of qualified healthcare provider

### Elemental Diet

- Liquid formula of pre-digested nutrients
- Starves bacteria while nourishing the body
- **73% normal breath tests** after two-week elemental diet
- **83% reported feeling better overall**
- Should only be done under medical supervision
- Duration: Typically 2-3 weeks

## Root Causes & Risk Factors

Understanding root causes is critical for preventing recurrence.

### Motility Disorders

The **Migrating Motor Complex (MMC)** is the primary defense mechanism:
- Wave-like contractions during fasting that "sweep" the small intestine clean
- Disrupted MMC is strongly associated with SIBO development
- Major hormonal regulator: Motilin

**Conditions affecting motility:**
- Gastroparesis
- Hypothyroidism
- Scleroderma and connective tissue disorders
- Parkinson's disease and neurological conditions
- Post-surgical changes
- Opioid medications

### Low Stomach Acid

**Normal stomach acid:**
- Creates barrier protecting gut from ingested micro-organisms
- Organisms normally killed in stomach before reaching small intestine

**Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs):**
- Multiple meta-analyses show higher SIBO frequency in PPI users
- SIBO documented in 13.2% of PPI users vs 1.8% when PPIs combined with prokinetics
- Even 7 days of PPI use (40mg pantoprazole) resulted in 7.8% SIBO incidence in healthy volunteers

### Structural Abnormalities

- Abdominal surgery (adhesions, altered anatomy)
- Gastric bypass surgery
- Strictures or fistulas
- Radiation damage
- Diverticula

### Associated Conditions

SIBO is commonly associated with:
- Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS)
- Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD/Crohn's)
- Celiac disease
- Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD)
- Cirrhosis
- Pancreatitis
- Chronic renal failure
- Diabetes

### The SIBO-GERD Connection

The relationship is often **bidirectional:**
1. **GERD → SIBO:** PPI treatment for reflux can predispose to SIBO
2. **SIBO → GERD:** Gas from bacterial overgrowth increases pressure on lower esophageal sphincter, causing reflux

Many patients treated for "GERD" may actually have underlying SIBO.

## Diet & Nutrition

### Low-FODMAP Diet

- **Most well-studied diet** for SIBO symptom management
- Improves symptoms in ~70% of IBS patients
- Works by reducing fermentable carbohydrates that feed bacteria
- Symptom improvement rates: 84-86%
- **Important:** Reduces symptoms but does not treat underlying overgrowth

**FODMAP = Fermentable Oligosaccharides, Disaccharides, Monosaccharides, and Polyols**

### Other Dietary Approaches

| Diet | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Specific Carbohydrate Diet (SCD)** | Eliminates complex carbohydrates |
| **SIBO Specific Diet** | Combination of SCD and low-FODMAP |
| **Bi-Phasic Diet** | Two-phase approach: restrictive then reintroduction |
| **Cedars-Sinai Diet** | Less restrictive approach |

### Key Principles

1. Diets primarily **reduce symptoms** by starving bacteria
2. Diets alone typically **do not eradicate SIBO**
3. Should be combined with antimicrobial treatment
4. Long-term restriction not recommended - work toward reintroduction

### Supportive Nutrients

- **Tryptophan:** Essential amino acid that accelerates intestinal transit and gastric emptying
- **Fiber and polyphenols:** Can support motility
- **B12 supplementation:** Often depleted in SIBO patients

## Motility & Prevention

### The Migrating Motor Complex (MMC)

**What it is:**
- Pattern of electrical and muscular activity in the gut during fasting
- "Housekeeper wave" that clears debris and bacteria from small intestine
- Occurs every 90-120 minutes during fasting

**Why it matters:**
- Absent or disordered MMC is strongly associated with SIBO
- Restoring MMC function is key to preventing recurrence

### Lifestyle Interventions for MMC

1. **Meal Spacing:** Allow 3-4 hours between meals (no snacking)
2. **Overnight Fasting:** Minimum 12 hours, especially for IBS/SIBO patients
3. **Stress Management:** Stress inhibits MMC activity
4. **Physical Activity:** Regular movement supports motility

### Prokinetic Medications

Prokinetics stimulate the MMC and prevent SIBO recurrence.

#### Pharmaceutical Prokinetics

| Medication | Notes |
|------------|-------|
| Low-dose erythromycin | Motilin receptor agonist |
| Low-dose naltrexone (LDN) | Off-label use |
| Prucalopride | 5-HT4 agonist |

#### Natural Prokinetics

| Supplement | Evidence |
|------------|----------|
| **Ginger** | Most studied; 2011 trial showed increased gastric emptying |
| **Iberogast** | Herbal combination; studied for functional dyspepsia |
| **Artichoke extract** | Limited evidence |
| **5-HTP** | Serotonin precursor |

**Note:** Herbal prokinetics may not be strong enough in all cases; pharmaceutical options may be needed.
